- **116-2 (2020)**: CBO https://www.cbo.gov/publication/57084. CARES + CAA-2021 account for ~80% of the $2.8T total. FFCRA and PPPHCEA already in 14. Remaining laws individually well below $25B.

- **117-2 (2022)**: CBO https://www.cbo.gov/publication/58956. PACT, CHIPS, IRA all in existing 14. - **118-1 (2023)**: CBO https://www.cbo.gov/publication/60157. FRA already in 14 (existing list cites -$1,500B for the discretionary cap savings; CBO's mandatory-only score in the 118-1 report is -$8.0B). NDAA FY24 (P.L. 118-31) scored at -$1.5B mandatory only. No new candidates.
- **118-2 (2024)**: CBO https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61239 (also 61197). SSFA dominates at +$196B (= E5). No other 118-2 law had |score| ≥ $25B. (Note: P.L. 119-4 FY25 CR with IRS rescission scored at +$66B but is in 119-1, = E6.)

Supplementals NOT counted: National security supplementals (e.g., P.L. 117-128 Ukraine $40B, P.L. 118-50 Ukraine/Israel/Indo-Pacific $95B) are EMERGENCY-DESIGNATED DISCRETIONARY appropriations. They do not appear in CBO's mandatory/revenue reports and would not be scored as mandatory legislation by the existing methodology. Similarly the FY19 disaster supplemental (P.L. 116-20, $19B), CR-attached disaster supplemental (P.L. 118-158, ~$110B BA) are discretionary. Flag for Aadit: if Variant E wants to include emergency supplementals on a separate basis, this would be a methodological extension.

## Open questions for Aadit

1. **F3 (Wyden-Smith / H.R. 7024)**: Include or exclude? Net JCT score ~$0.4B fails the $25B threshold, but gross policy magnitude is ~$78-79B on each side (CTC expansion + ERC offset). My recommendation: EXCLUDE on strict-net-deficit basis to match the methodology used for the existing 14 (which all use net 10-yr CBO score). Left in the table as F3 with a flag so you can decide.
2. **F6 (H.R. 3762 / 2015 ACA repeal)**: Two House votes (Oct 23 2015 initial passage / Roll 568; Jan 6 2016 motion to concur on Senate amendment / Roll 6). The Jan 6 2016 vote is on the final form that went to the President. Recommendation: cite the Jan 6 2016 Roll 6 vote. Also: cite -$282B (conventional / non-macro) or -$474B (with macro feedback)? The existing 14 use conventional CBO scores, so default to -$282B and note the macro figure.
3. **F2 (AHCA)**: Should we also separately include the Senate "skinny repeal" (Roll 179, July 28 2017, scored -$179B)? Different score, different chamber, different version of H.R. 1628. If yes, list as F2b.
4. **E2 (BBA 2015) sign convention**: The bill's mandatory/revenue side is net DEFICIT-REDUCING (~-$80B); discretionary caps were RAISED by $80B. Existing 14 records BBA 2018 (+$320B) using the discretionary-cap-raise sign. For consistency, should E2's score be reported as -$80B (mandatory/revenue, matching CBO's mandatory reports) or +$80B (discretionary caps, matching BBA-2018 convention)? Same question applies to E4 (FAST Act).
5. **E6 (FY25 CR + IRS rescission, P.L. 119-4)**: The +$66B score comes entirely from CBO's "reduced enforcement → lower collections" mechanism. This is the same mechanism that earned P.L. 118-47 a +$38B score (rejected from the candidate list per the previous agent's notes). Apply the same exclusion rule to E6? Recommendation: KEEP E6 because it's primary policy-driven (the rescission is the substantive legislative choice), but flag the methodological inconsistency.
6. **Gap-fill enacted bills**: Confirmed zero new candidates beyond E1–E6. If Variant E wants to expand to emergency supplementals (Ukraine/Israel/disaster), that's a methodological extension that would add ~5-6 more laws (P.L. 117-128 $40B, P.L. 118-50 $95B, P.L. 118-158 Div B ~$110B, etc.) — but they're all discretionary BA, not mandatory/revenue. Aadit's call.
